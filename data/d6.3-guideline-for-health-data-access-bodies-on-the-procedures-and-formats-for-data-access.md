Guideline for health data access bodies on the procedures and formats for data access
1 Executive summary
In the scope of the second joint action Towards the European Health Data Space (TEHDAS2), this
guideline elaborates in detail the different processes that health data access bodies (HDABs) go
through in order to implement their responsibilities under Articles 67–69, 72 and 73 of the European
Health Data Space (EHDS) regulation (2025/327): to manage and assess health data access
applications and health data requests, to issue data permits and approve health data requests, to
obtain data from health data holders, to interact with trusted health data holders, to cooperate with
health data users and to enable data access to facilitate the secondary use of health data.
As such, the guideline focuses on three distinct stages in the workflow: the necessary application
pre-screening that is followed by the thorough assessment and evaluation of the applications and
requests. Subsequently, the guideline briefly describes what takes place after a data permit has
been granted or a data request approved.
Pre-screening of health data access applications and health data requests is the necessary first step
to verify that all required information is provided and filled out properly, without prejudging the legal
admissibility or scientific validity of the application. The checks described in this guideline aim to
ensure technical completeness, semantically valid input and contextual consistency of the
application.
Following the application pre-screening, the assessment process - to decide whether a data permit
should be issued, or a health data request approved - is comprehensively described. This guideline
sets out the requirements that must be met for information included in an application to be considered
complete, in accordance with the EHDS Regulation. During the assessment of the application, and
for the purposes of issuing a data permit or approving a data request, this guideline emphasises the
fulfilment of the specific criteria that are outlined in the EHDS Regulation. It should be noted that the
suggested framework allows Member States to define and operationalise their own specific
processes, provided they remain within the boundaries of the EHDS Regulation. A concise overview
of the procedural time frame is also provided in the document.
Finally, the guideline briefly describes the actions undertaken by the HDAB that follow the decision,
in coordination with health data holders and health data users. The decision is either a data permit,
a data request approval, or a refusal. The guideline briefly outlines the coordination of secure data
extraction and transmission, the data preparation, making data available in secure processing
environments (SPEs) and communicating with the SPEs. Additionally, the guideline also provides
basic information of administrative processes such as invoicing for the provided services, the
continuous monitoring of compliance and documentation processes. Especially the actions to be
taken after the decision are largely in the scope of other TEHDAS2 documents and in such cases
the relevant document is referred to.
This guideline serves as operational guidance for HDABs describing all the procedural steps from
receiving an application, to issuing a decision, to actions to be conducted after the decision. In doing
so, it ensures alignment with both the EHDS Regulation and the General Data Protection Regulation
(GDPR).

3 Introduction
Advancing health data use in the European Health Union
As part of the European Health Union, the European Union (EU) is advancing the use of health data
for secondary purposes, including research, innovation and policymaking. Smooth and secure
access to data will drive the development of new treatments and medicines and optimise resource
utilisation – all with the overarching goal of improving the health of citizens across Europe.
TEHDAS2 represents a significant step forward in this vision. The project will develop guidelines and
technical specifications to facilitate smooth cross-border use of health data and support health data
holders, health data users and the new HDABs in fulfilling their responsibilities and obligations
outlined in the EHDS Regulation.
TEHDAS2 focuses on several critical aspects of health data use.
• Data discovery: findability and availability of health data, ensuring it is accessible for secondary
purposes.
• Data access: developing harmonised access procedures and establishing standardised
approaches for granting data access across member states.
• Secure processing environment: defining technical specifications for environments where
sensitive health data can be processed safely.
• Citizen-centric obligations: providing guidance on fulfilling obligations to citizens, such as
communicating significant research findings that impact their health, informing them about
research outcomes and ensuring transparency in how their data are used.
• Collaboration models: developing guidance on collaboration and guidelines on fees and
penalties as well as third country and international access to data.
TEHDAS2 will contribute to harmonised implementation of the EHDS Regulation through the
concrete guidelines and technical specifications. Some of these documents and resources will also
provide input to implementing acts of the regulation. Hence, the joint action will increase the
preparedness for the EHDS implementation and lead to better coordination of member states’ joint
efforts towards the secondary use of health data, while also reducing fragmentation in policies and
practices related to secondary use.
The EHDS Regulation and health data access bodies
Secondary use of electronic health data can bring great benefits, but it is important that datasets are
accessible, of high quality and suitable for the purpose of creating scientific, innovative and societal
value, whilst at the same time all the necessary safeguards are in place to ensure data protection,
safety, confidentiality and mitigation of risks.
Without hindering or replacing existing mechanisms or contractual agreements, the EHDS
Regulation establishes a common framework to access electronic health data for secondary use and
for the purposes defined in the regulation. Under this framework, health data holders should make
the data they hold available based on a data permit or a health data request approval. To
operationalise the requirements, one or more HDABs shall be appointed in each member state, data
processing shall take place in an SPE (in the case of a data permit) and requirements for proper
data processing shall be set out in the data permits and data request approvals.

Therefore, the main responsibilities of an HDAB in the scope of this guideline, according to Article
57 of the EHDS Regulation, include:
• deciding on health data access applications pursuant to Article 67 of the regulation, authorising
and issuing data permits pursuant to Article 68 to access electronic health data and deciding on
health data requests pursuant to Article 69, also in accordance with Chapter II of Regulation (EU)
2022/868,
• maintaining a management system to record and process health data access applications, health
data requests, data permits issued and health data requests approved,
• publishing metadata catalogues on the source and nature of electronic health data available at
national level, in accordance with Articles 77, 78 and 80,
• requesting electronic health data referred to in Article 51 from relevant health data holders
pursuant to a data permit issued or a health data request approved,
• receiving, combining, preparing and compiling the requested datasets as defined in the granted
permit or request approval, and ensuring that anonymisation or pseudonymisation measures are
applied as appropriate,
• preserving the confidentiality of intellectual property rights, for regulatory data protection and of
trade secrets as provided for in Article 52, considering the relevant rights of both the health data
holder and health data user,
• providing access to electronic health data to health data users pursuant to a data permit in an
SPE in accordance with Article 73,
• maintaining and managing public information systems to record the handling of health data
applications and requests as well as measures related to non-compliance pursuant to Article 63,
to publish the results communicated by health data users pursuant to Article 61(4) and to inform
the citizens on the conditions under which data are made available and on how their rights are
protected and safeguarded according to Article 58,
• monitoring and supervising compliance of health data users and health data holders with the
requirements laid down in the regulation and coordinating with supervisory authorities where
necessary,
• facilitating cross-border access to electronic health data for secondary use hosted in other
member states through HealthData@EU referred to in Article 75 and cooperating closely with
each other and with the Commission.

The guideline is intended for the following audiences:
HDABs: To provide practical guidance for implementing their responsibilities under Articles 67–69,

applications and requests, issue permits and perform required actions after the decision.
Health data applicants (e.g. researchers, public authorities): To explain how different aspects of

3.2 Purpose
The purpose of this document is to support the harmonised implementation of the EHDS Regulation
across member states, proposing a consistent way of handling health data access applications or
health data requests, issuing data permits or health data request approvals and enabling the
secondary use of health data.
More specifically, the document provides guidelines on the procedures involved in the entire lifecycle
of secondary use of health data - starting with the pre-screening of submitted data access
applications or health data requests, to a consistent and methodological approach for their
assessment process. This includes validating that health data applicants have only applied for data
that are adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the declared purpose of
use. The guideline will introduce both mandatory tasks of the HDAB according to the EHDS
Regulation, as well as provide practical suggestions.
In addition, the actions following approval are also addressed, including data extraction, data
sharing, monitoring of compliance and publication of research outputs. In most cases, the described
actions following approval are covered more in depth in other TEHDAS2 documents and in these
cases the relevant document is appropriately referred to.
Please note that this guideline and especially its chapters 5 and 6 related to application pre-
screening and assessment, should be read together with the application forms (templates in annexes
5 and 6). These chapters of the guideline act as a handbook for application processing and they are
not fully self-explanatory.
This document should be understood as an expert opinion and guidance document developed within
the TEHDAS2 framework, reflecting technical and expert input from the project partners. It is not
legally binding and does not constitute a formal guideline or technical specification under the
European Health Data Space.
This document does not represent the position of the European Commission.
Legally binding and enforceable requirements under the European Health Data Space are laid down
in Regulation (EU) 2025/327 and, where applicable, in Implementing Acts adopted by the European
Commission, within the limits of the empowerments provided by the Regulation.

4 Scope
4.1 Scope of the process
This guideline is part of a series developed under TEHDAS2 project to operationalise the EHDS
Regulation, specifically addressing Chapter IV on secondary use of health data. The scope of this
guideline begins after the data discovery phase, once the applicant has identified the datasets of
interest in the EU or national dataset catalogue and has submitted a health data access application
or a health data request via the central platform or HDAB’s own application system. This guideline
is based on the templates for the health data access application, health data request, data permit
and health data request approval that are available in the HealthData@EU Central Platform. These
templates are annexed to this guideline (annexes 5, 6, 9 and 10 respectively). In this guideline, the
term “application form” refers to both health data access applications and health data requests.
When the guideline refers to only one of these, it is clearly stated.
This guideline focuses on the role of the HDAB and provides an overview of the entire workflow in
the health data access application and the health data request journey. It focuses on the operational
processes required to enable health data sharing according to Chapter IV of the EHDS Regulation
– from the issuance of data permits and data request approvals to the data processing. Where
applicable, this guideline and recommended procedures also apply to trusted health data holders,
when they are fulfilling their duties under Article 72 and to the European Commission’s Union Health
Data Access Service (UHDAS), when the health data holders are EU institutions, bodies, offices or
agencies in line with Article 56. Overall, the document serves as the operational interpretation of
Articles 67 to 69, 72 and 73, providing guidance to support the EHDS implementation.
As already mentioned, the guideline recommends that a pre-screening of the information provided
in the health data access applications or health data requests should precede the assessment of the
necessary criteria to grant a permit or approve a request. The pre-screening serves as a checkpoint
where it is verified that all mandatory information has been submitted successfully: it is either
uploaded as an attachment, in the format of free text or as selection from check lists. This cannot be
done entirely automatically. The pre-screening is a formal validation step - not a substantive review
- and must not include judgements on content adequacy or project quality. This distinction is
essential for legal clarity and efficiency. Both processes, the pre-screening and the assessment that
leads either to a refusal or to a data permit / data request approval, are outlined and operationalised
in detail, with proper references to the relevant EHDS provisions.
Finally, the guideline lists the post-approval processes that involve for example data extraction and
secure, transparent and appropriate data sharing, continuous compliance monitoring mechanisms,
administrative mechanisms and dissemination of outcomes.
The methodology for developing this deliverable is elaborated in Annex 1 and the conducted public
consultation round and its outcomes are discussed in Annex 2.

5 Application pre-screening
The pre-screening aims to help the HDAB verify that each application is complete and filled out
properly. Health data applicants can apply for different types of health data:
• Health data access applications, for access to anonymised or pseudonymised individual-level
electronic health data within an SPE, and
• Health data requests, for access to anonymised, aggregated (non-individual-level) statistics.
The EHDS Regulation defines specific time frames for providing a response to the applicant [Articles
68(4) and 69(4)]. It is therefore important to identify any missing information or deficiencies early in
the process and avoid losing time with processing incomplete applications. Hence, the pre-screening
aims to help the HDAB verify that each application is complete and filled out properly.
While the pre-screening is not explicitly mentioned in the EHDS Regulation, it mandates the
assessment of health data requests for their completeness [Article 69(3)] and defines the
completeness of a health data access application as a prerequisite for issuing a data permit [Article
68(4)]. Therefore, we can conclude that data access applications and data requests must be pre-
screened for completeness.
These applications can either concern data from a single member state (‘national application’) or
data from multiple member states (‘multi-country application’). National applications will be submitted
through the national Data Access Application Management system (DAAMs) and multi-country
applications through the HealthData@EU Central Platform (HD@EU CP). In the case of multi-
country applications, the applications are routed to respective national contact points and
subsequently to the country-specific HDAB(s) who perform(s) their own pre-screening
independently.
The pre-screening ensures that an application is technically complete and that all fields are filled with
appropriate type of data. This step does not assess the quality or scientific value of the application
but only aims to verify that required fields are filled with plausible content, enabling the application
to move forward to the actual assessment phase.
The HDAB is advised to pre-screen a health data access application or a health data request for
completeness upon receipt. Only applications that have successfully undergone the pre-screening
will move to the application assessment phase. Performing the pre-screening must not lead to undue
delays in line with the time limits defined in Article 68(4) of the EHDS Regulation and further
discussed in Chapter 8 of this document.
Application form templates are attached to this guideline: Annex 5 for health data access application
and Annex 6 for health data request, respectively. To enable automation, reduce administrative
burden and allow applicants to pre-populate forms, machine-readable solutions may be used -
further developments of the HealthData@EU central platform can be expected, but HDABs may
introduce national solutions to make the application form templates machine-readable.
Please refer to Annexes 7 and 8 for checklists for pre-screening the health data access application
and health data request, respectively. These act as helpful tools for the pre-screening.
As a summary, the distinction between the pre-screening and the formal application assessment
may be presented as follows:

• Verify that all required fields and documents are present and technically valid.
• Prevents delays by ensuring applications are not rejected for missing information

5.1 Making applications public
The EHDS Regulation mandates that the HDAB shall make public, through electronic means, any
health data access application and health data request without undue delay after initial reception
[Article 57(1)(j)(ii)]. The publication should occur as soon as possible after the application is received,
even if it is later found to be incomplete and requiring amendments. This means that the publication
takes place prior to the pre-screening. Regardless of whether the application is submitted through
the national DAAMs portal (for a national application) or via the HD@EU CP (but concerns a
dataset under that HDAB), the HDAB has the obligation to make the application submission public.
Updated versions or changes to the application introduced later during the pre-screening and
assessment phases can be published as updates to the same entry.
The purpose of publishing applications is to provide transparency. The HDAB must be aware that
applications can contain e.g. contact information, banking details, trade secrets and information
under intellectual property rights (IPR). The applicant will be able to specify the information that is
considered confidential and provide the appropriate justification in the application forms. Therefore,
the HDAB will be able to review the confidentiality request and decide not to publish certain
information.
5.2 Defining a ‘complete application’ ready for processing
In the EHDS Regulation, a ‘complete application’ is not defined. Completeness aims for a minimum
level of information provided in the application. A ‘complete application’ is one where all required
information is provided, is semantically meaningful and is contextually consistent. The pre-screening
should be understood as sanity checks (e.g. detecting clearly nonsensical entries or missing
attachments) and not as in-depth assessment of relevance or sufficiency, which is outside the scope
of this phase. The checks can aim:
(i) to confirm that attachments can be opened, are not empty or clearly incomplete.
(ii) to verify that data are logically correct and meaningful within the context of a health data
access application or health data request. For example, text fields (e.g. project name) contain
real words (not random characters), numerical fields (e.g. phone number) contain valid
values, address fields contain text that resembles a real address format, etc.
(iii) to ensure that the provided input fits within the expected content, i.e. there is cross-field
consistency. This check is aimed to identifying illogical or contradictory issues in the
application, not to evaluate e.g. the project rationale for being scientifically sound. For
example, to ensure that the ‘From’ date precedes the ‘To’ date in the defined data processing
period.

5.3 Pre-screening steps
Upon receiving a health data access application or a health data request, the HDAB is advised to
run a pre-screening before proceeding to the assessment phase. The DAAMs or the HD@EU CP
shall ensure that every mandatory field of the application is filled in before it can be submitted.
The pre-screening involves additional validation/verification tasks, many of which can be automated
by the data management systems. National DAAMs are encouraged to implement format checks,
semi-automated validations and automated plausibility controls for selected fields. For example, the
following fields are potential candidates for automated validation (IBAN, VAT number, Operator ID,
Business ID) as they follow known national or European formatting rules.
However, the substantive quality or relevance of the information provided cannot be assessed
automatically. Hence, the pre-screening step is recommended to ensure completeness in terms of
plausibility of values in the input fields. It should be verified that the necessary information is provided
and is relevant for the specific application type in question, in accordance with Article 67(2) and
Article 69(2) for health data access applications and health data requests, respectively.
It is advisable to scan through all mandatory fields from the health data access application form or
health data request form. Common plausibility pre-screening checks are presented in Table 1 below.

5.5 Results of the pre-screening and next actions
If the health data access application is incomplete, the HDAB shall notify the applicant through the
communication channel in use. If the applicant does not complete the application within the given
time frame of four weeks, a data permit shall not be issued [Article 68(4)]. In such cases HDAB may
reject the application due to its incompleteness or the applicant’s unresponsiveness. This rejection
before actual formal assessment constitutes a procedural decision rather than a substantive refusal
and therefore does not require a formal decision document. Nevertheless, the rejection must be
justified and properly documented. Although a similar response time frame of four weeks is not
explicitly foreseen in the EHDS Regulation for health data requests (as opposed to health data
access applications), there is no prohibition either. It is therefore possible to apply a similar four-
week window to allow modifications or completion of data requests. This would be beneficial for
consistency and user experience and can be adopted operationally.
When the application is deemed complete, the HDAB should check whether it concerns only data
from one trusted data holder. If that is the case, the assessment will follow the process described in
Article 72 and in Chapter 6.3 of this guideline.

6.7 Cross-border and multi-country cases
It is possible for applicants to request access to multiple datasets from different member states or
from cross-border registries and databases. When applying for datasets from multiple countries, the
applicant must apply via the HealthData@EU Central Platform. Applications will be distributed to the
national contact points, which will then forward them to the relevant national HDAB(s).
For cross-border registries or databases, the HDAB responsible for overseeing the health data
holder with which the registry or database is registered shall decide on the health data access
application. Where such registries or databases have joint controllers, the HDAB responsible for
granting access shall be that of one of the member states in which a joint controller is established
[Article 76(1)].
Only one HDAB shall be responsible for granting access to a jointly held dataset, in order to avoid
duplicate or conflicting decisions.
Where registries or databases from several member states organise themselves into a single EU-
level network, they may designate a coordinator. In such cases, the HDAB of the Member state in
which the coordinator is established shall be responsible for granting access to the data [Article 76
(2)].
When a health data applicant requests datasets from multiple Member states, each HDAB or
authorised participant is responsible for the decision to grant or refuse access to electronic health
data within its own scope of responsibility [Article 68(5)]. Therefore, in multi-country cases the
applicant will receive multiple decisions to grant or refuse access to the data. Given the complexity
of multi-country cases, HDABs may benefit from consulting other HDABs or competent authorities
before issuing a data permit or health data request approval. HDABs and authorised participants in
HD@EU shall inform each other of their decisions granting access to electronic health data.
Additionally, other HDABs may take that information into consideration when deciding on granting
or refusing access to electronic health data. A data permit issued by one HDAB may benefit from
mutual recognition by the other HDABs, allowing for more informed and consistent evaluations
[Article 68(5)]. Mutual recognition is possible but not automatic or mandatory.
In multi-country cases, the multiple datasets can be combined within a single SPE. HDABs may
decide to provide access to electronic health data in the SPE provided by one of the HDABs or the
European Commission [Articles 68(8) and 75(9)]. If one HDAB issues its permit earlier than the
others, the corresponding dataset may be made available in the SPE immediately, allowing the
applicant to access it before the remaining datasets. Once the remaining permits are issued, the
additional datasets may be added to the SPE. However, according to Article 75(9), the use of the
European Commission’s SPE requires a request from at least two member states’ HDABs.
Therefore, at least two HDABs must have issued positive decisions before any data may be made
available in the European Commission’s SPE.
Information on third-country data access applications or data requests are available in the TEHDAS2
document “D4.3 Guidelines on international and third-country access and transfer of health data”.

the application of any permit conditions, the provision of access to authorised data, and oversight
during the project’s duration. These steps are presented as points 5-7 in the end-to-end process
overview in Figure 1.
7.1 Extraction requests for health data holders
After the data permit or the health data request has been accepted by the health data applicant, the
HDAB must immediately request data extraction from the respective health data holder(s) as per
Articles 60 and 68(7) if not otherwise agreed with the health data user or stipulated in the permit.
However, where duly justified, the initiation of data extraction may be deferred, for example where
the requested data are not yet available or where the health data user requests a postponement due
to e.g. operational or funding-related constraints. In such cases, the health data user shall inform the
HDAB or the relevant data holder of the intended timing for initiating the extraction. Where the health
data user does not request the initiation of data extraction, no extraction shall take place, and this
shall be appropriately recorded.
The request should include:
• A copy of the approved data permit or the health data request approval
• A clear specification of the required datasets, including scope, format and intended use
• Deadline for extracting the data. Data holders must provide requested data to the HDAB within
three months, though this deadline can be extended by another three months in justified cases.
Further information about data extraction requests for health data holders can be found in the
TEHDAS2 document “D6.1 Guideline for health data holders on making personal and non-personal
electronic health data available for reuse”.
7.2 Data preparation, sharing and the SPE
After the extraction requests have been sent, the following steps take place:
• Health data holders must put the requested electronic health data at the disposal of the HDAB
in accordance with the data permit. Effective data preparation relies on precise definitions agreed
upon during the application phase. However, in the case of the requirements of health data user,
a technical dialogue between the health data user and health data holder is allowed to clarify
specific technical formats.
• Data Extraction: The HDAB must be check that the datasets received from health data holders
are technically intact and in accordance with the description of the requested data, e.g. not
including any data beyond those permitted.
• Data Formatting and Processing: The extracted data are processed and formatted by the
HDAB to ensure compliance with technical and ethical standards, including harmonisation where
it is required and de-identification procedures such as anonymisation or pseudonymisation in
accordance with Article 57(1)(b). Some of these actions (e.g. extraction cleaning) might be
conducted by the health data holder prior to delivery. These actions must be performed under
the supervision of the HDAB. Formatting data to ensure compliance can be delegated to the

health data holder or trusted data holder. Further information on the steps related specifically to
trusted health data holders is given in Chapter 7.9 of this document. Further information about
how data can be linked and steps preceding it are specified in the document ''D7.5 Guideline on
linkage of health datasets'' and further information on data processing measures in TEHDAS2
document “D7.2 Guideline for Health Data Access Bodies on data minimisation,
pseudonymisation, anonymisation and synthetic data”.
• Data Encryption and Security Measures: Before transferring data to the SPE, the HDAB must
implement encryption and access control mechanisms to prevent unauthorised access, following
Article 57(1)(b). Secure logging of data transmission activities must be maintained for audit
purposes. The SPE should ensure compliance with the security measures as specified in Article
73(1) to prevent unauthorised access and mitigate potential security threats.
• Data Transfer to the SPE (only applicable in case of a data permit): The HDAB securely
transfers the prepared dataset to the designated SPE, ensuring compliance with the EHDS
Regulation’s security requirements of SPEs as specified in Article 73. HDAB shall also review
the data to ensure they are in the format and scope specified in the data permit before transfer
to the SPE. Further guidance on making data available in the SPEs by the HDABs can be found
in the TEHDAS2 document “D7.4 Technical, functional and security specifications of secure
processing environments”.
• Delivery of the result to the applicant (i.e. anonymised and aggregated outputs; only
applicable in case of a health data request): Health data request approval does not require the
data to be released into an SPE: the data can be delivered directly to the health data user.
• Deletion of data from the SPE must take place within six months of expiry of the related data
permit, following Article 68(12).
• Result extraction: The health data user must contact the HDAB if they wish to download any
anonymised, statistical data from the SPE. The HDAB controls and determines which data (if
any) are allowed to leave the SPE.
• The health data user may opt to store authorised datasets in storage systems with reduced
processing capabilities for non-active use, where available, subject to compliance with applicable
security and access requirements. Such arrangements may be subject to differentiated technical
conditions and fees.

7.3 Formula used for the creation of the dataset
Article 68(12) states that “At the request of the health data user, the formula for the creation of the
requested dataset may be stored by the health data access body”. This can facilitate replication of
studies by the same or different researchers. National HDABs should establish their own procedures
for engagement with health data users on retention of these formulas. The formula for creation of
the dataset could include detailed information related to extraction processes conducted by the
health data holder. These data are not necessarily routinely retained. Consequently, it is important
that the HDAB and health data holders are aware early in the process that the formula related to a
particular application is to be retained. This is requested in the application form templates (Annexes
9 and 10).

7.4 Invoicing
HDABs, the UHDAS and trusted health data holders are entitled to charge fees according to the
EHDS Regulation. The TEHDAS2 document “D4.1.1 Guideline on fees related to the EHDS
Regulation” discusses further the different scenarios and provides recommendations for invoicing.
7.5 Communication with the SPE
Permitted and prepared datasets are securely made available to the health data user within the SPE.
This takes place through the HDAB, which will provide the users with credentials to identify them as
being authorised to access the data within the SPE referred to in the data permit. SPE operator
provides dedicated technical support, discussed in detail in the TEHDAS2 document “D7.4
Technical, functional and security specifications of secure processing environments”. All
communication must be monitored and logged by the HDAB. The process must comply with Articles
57(1)(a)(i) and 73(1). This is further specified in the TEHDAS2 document “D7.1 Guideline on how to
use data in a secure processing environment”.
7.6 Monitoring
The HDAB must continuously monitor data usage, security and compliance with the conditions set
out in the data permit as required by Article 57(1)(a)(ii). Regular audits and security assessments
should be performed to remain in compliance with all the security provisions in Article 73. A range of
established standards and recommendations relevant to data management, information security,
privacy, and compliance - such as DAMA-DMBOK and the ISO/IEC 27000 series - can be used to
support monitoring activities in the EHDS context.
Processed datasets must be retained only for the period required to fulfil their intended research
purpose. Secure disposal procedures must be implemented in compliance with Article 73 to mitigate
risks associated with unauthorised data retention. Further information is provided in the TEHDAS2
document “D7.4 Technical, functional and security specifications of secure processing
environments”. Monitoring should also be integrated to HD@EU services based on requirements in
Article 96. Further information is provided in the TEHDAS2 document “D7.3 Technical specifications
for Health Data Access Bodies on the implementation of the common IT infrastructure".
Any data breaches or misuse must be reported within the GDPR data breach notification timelines
to the health data holders along with relevant authorities and documented in accordance with EHDS
guidelines and GDPR requirements. Moreover, any use of large language models or AI for
processing of data in the SPE must comply with the purpose of use and scope specified in the data
permit.
Monitoring should also be aligned with the TEHDAS2 document ”D4.1.2 Draft guideline on penalties
for non-compliance related to the EHDS Regulation”, which provides practical support to the HDABs
in exercising their supervisory and enforcement powers under Articles 63 and 64.

• The number and type of health data access applications and health data requests processed
• Compliance measures undertaken

• Improvements and future policy recommendations

7.8 Revocation of permit
HDABs have the power to revoke a data permit in cases of non-compliance by a health data user
[Article 63(3)].
HDABs should be aware that non-compliance can be in relation to any requirement placed on health
data users by Chapter IV of the EHDS Regulation and is not limited to misuse of data. It could, for
example, relate to non-payment of permit extension fees.
Revocation of a permit will necessarily involve a notification to the health data user. Where the HDAB
decides to revoke a data permit, it should also inform the health data holder and the SPE provider
or otherwise take steps to cease processing. When making these notifications to the SPE provider,
the health data user and the health data holder, the HDAB does not have to divulge the reason for
revocation – the HDAB should exercise its own judgement on this point.
Enforcement measures taken by the HDAB, including revocation of a data permit, must be notified
to other HDABs through the IT tool referred to in Article 63(7). Further information on the enforcement
measures and processes can be found in the TEHDAS2 document “D4.1.2 Guideline on penalties
related to the EHDS Regulation”.
7.9 Steps after decision when trusted heath data holder is involved
Note to reader: The Chapter 7.9 sets out the procedural steps and responsibilities that apply after a
data permit has been issued or a health data request has been approved and where a trusted health
data holder is involved in the provision of electronic health data for secondary use. It reflects the
obligations and safeguards applicable following a decision, including data preparation, access
provision, monitoring, and termination of access, as established under Articles 72 and 74 of the
EHDS Regulation.
Preparation and provision of data by the trusted health data holder
Following the granting of a data permit or health data request approval, the trusted health data holder
shall prepare the electronic health data strictly in accordance with the scope, purpose, and conditions
specified in the decision. In carrying out the preparation and provision of data, the trusted health data
holder shall act as a data controller for determining which electronic health data are disclosed and
for ensuring that only the data authorised under the permit or request approval are included.
The trusted health data holder shall apply data minimisation measures and ensure that the data
provided are relevant to the authorised purpose and technically suitable for processing within the
designated SPE. The trusted health data holder shall make the prepared data available either to the
HDAB or directly within the SPE, as specified in the decision.

Provision of access through an SPE
Where access to electronic health data is provided through an SPE, the trusted health data holder
shall enable access solely for the persons authorised under the data permit. In such cases, the
trusted health data holder shall act as a data processor on behalf of the health data user, executing
access strictly in line with the conditions set out in the data permit.
The trusted health data holder shall ensure that the SPE prevents unauthorised access, extraction,
or re-identification of personal electronic health data and enforces appropriate technical and
organisational access controls.
Cooperation with the HDAB
For the duration of the access granted, the trusted health data holder shall cooperate with the HDAB
by providing information necessary to verify compliance with the data permit and responding to
requests related to data quality, security, or access conditions.
Any identified deviation from the conditions of the data permit or the health data request approval
shall be communicated without undue delay to the HDAB.
Monitoring, restriction, and termination of access
The trusted health data holder shall monitor compliance with the conditions stipulated in the data
permit for the entire duration of access. Where required, the trusted health data holder shall take
appropriate measures to restrict, suspend, or terminate access, including disabling credentials or
access pathways within the secure processing environment.
Upon expiry, suspension, or withdrawal of the data permit, the trusted health data holder shall ensure
that access to the data is revoked and that no further processing takes place beyond what is legally
required.
Accountability and documentation
The trusted health data holder shall document the actions taken following the decision, including the
data made available, the access controls applied and any incidents or corrective measures.
Such documentation shall be retained for compliance and audit purposes and made available to the
HDAB upon request.
7.10 Appeal process
All final decisions taken by the HDAB under the EHDS Regulation can be subject to appeal, either
by the affected health data applicant, health data user or health data holder. National administrative
law of the member state where the HDAB is established applies to the appeal process, including
procedural rules and jurisdiction. If the decision to be appealed has been made by the UHDAS, the
Court of Justice of the EU has the jurisdiction. HDABs shall include national appeal instructions as
an annex to each decision as a good administrative practice.
7.11 Amendments to permit
Health data users can apply for amendments to a permit after it has been issued [Articles 68(12) and
68(13)]. An amendment process is appropriate where only limited elements change and only those
elements are reassessed (for example, replacement of personnel, extension of the permit validity
period, changes in access rights, or minor technical adjustments). A new permit process would be

The health data user must provide justifications for any amendment to the permit. In general, the
responsibility should be on health data users to provide comprehensive information at the time of

results or output of secondary use must be published within 18 months after the completion of data
processing in the SPE or upon receiving the response to the health data request, as mandated by

Users must inform the HDAB from which they obtained the data permit about their results and
support the HDAB in making this information publicly available, ensuring transparency and

HDABs should establish procedures for engagement with health data users about sharing these

8 Time frames
8.1 Time frame for pre-screening
The EHDS Regulation does not set a time frame for the pre-screening. However, it sets time frames
for granting a data permit and on assessing a health data request.
The starting point for calculating time frames begins upon receiving a complete health data access
application. From the HDAB’s perspective, this means that prior work is needed in terms of ensuring
that the application is complete and ready to be processed. Experience has shown that a pre-
screening is beneficial for optimising resources for all parties in the process. Although for health data
requests there is no such provision for completeness in the EHDS Regulation, it would be beneficial
for consistency to process the health data requests in a similar fashion to health data access
applications.
HDABs may set their own target time (e.g. within 5-10 working days) for the pre-screening. While
these internal targets are not legally binding, they are useful for internal planning and resource
management.
The EHDS Regulation gives time frames for the applicant to complete missing or invalid information
during the pre-screening. The HDAB should provide a clear list of missing or incoherent items to the
health data applicant and if possible, list all these items at once. The health data applicant has up to
four weeks to provide the required information, according to Article 68(4). If the information is not
provided on time, the HDAB is not required to proceed with the assessment. The four-week period
is a single, continuous window.
8.2 Time frame for application assessment
In principle, a formal data permit, data request approval or refusal shall be given within three months
of receiving a complete application. In case of delay, the applicant shall be informed of the reason
for the delay. The HDAB may extend the period for responding to a health data access application
by three additional months where necessary, considering the urgency and complexity of the health
data access application and the volume of submitted applications.
The applicant can be asked to provide missing information also during the assessment process. The
HDAB may set a time frame for the applicant to respond (e.g. two weeks), although the EHDS does
not give one. This time frame can also be agreed on member state level. This same time frame can
be used when the cost estimate is sent to the health data applicant for confirmation. The three-month
assessment period is suspended while additional information is being requested from or provided by
the health data applicant during the assessment phase. The EHDS Regulation does not limit the
number of times the HDAB may contact the applicant during the assessment process. Requests for
additional information may therefore be made more than once, where necessary. However, if the
information provided remains insufficient to properly assess the application, the HDAB may conclude
the assessment with a formal refusal, in accordance with the EHDS Regulation. Further
specifications e.g. for handling prolonged applicant inactivity will be addressed in future guidance to
be given by the EHDS Board.
An accelerated health data access application procedure shall be provided for public sector bodies
and Union institutions, bodies, offices and agencies with a legal mandate in the field of public health,
if the processing of electronic health data is to be carried out for the purposes reserved for these
actors [Article 53(1): a. public interest in the areas of public or occupational health, b. policy-making
and regulatory activities, c. statistics]. In such cases, the data permit shall be formally issued or
refused within two months of receiving a complete application. The HDAB may extend the period for

responding to such health data access application by one additional month where necessary [Article
68(4)]. Therefore, these applications need to be recognised early in the process to make sure the
accelerated timeline can be kept.
Regarding health data requests, the HDAB must assess (and approve or refuse) the health data
request within three months of receiving it.
When an HDAB has forwarded a health data access application or health data request to a trusted
health data holder for assessment, the trusted health data holder submits their assessment and
proposal to the HDAB within two months of receiving the application. The HDAB must issue a
decision within two months of receiving the assessment.
In line with Recital 74 of the EHDS Regulation, HDABs are not required to follow a strict first-in-first-
out approach when processing applications. Prioritisation may be applied based on objective and
transparent criteria, such as:
- the urgency of the data use (e.g. related to public health emergencies),
- the public interest value of the request,
- the available capacity of the HDAB and complexity of the application.
It is therefore important to design internal procedures that allow identifying high-priority applications
early and allocating resources accordingly, while ensuring overall fairness and transparency.

Annex 2 Public consultation summary
A draft version of this document was in public consultation in October-November 2025. This
document was commented in total for 67 times. The number of responses may contain some
duplicates as there was no individual identification and verification required to respond to the
surveys. Some respondents have also responded both from data holder’s and data user’s
perspective. The responses came from 13 different countries from the EU countries and the
European Economic Area countries. Responses from Easter and Southern European countries were
largely missing. The respondents were primarily from three main types of organisations, listed in
order of prevalence: public organisations, academic/research organisations and private
organisations. The feedback received from stakeholders during the public consultation informed both
the content of the guideline and the decisions on which elements should be addressed within its
scope.
Stakeholders emphasised the need to clarify pre-screening phase’s purpose, role and timelines. In
response, the guideline now states that the pre-screening is a technical verification step, intended to
confirm that applications contains all the information required under the EHDS Regulation and that
the information provided is technically valid. As a technical task, it may be automated to the extent
possible through national DAAMs. Indicative timeframes for performing the pre-screening are
proposed, while recognising that the EHDS Regulation does not prescribe specific deadlines for this
step and that national implementation arrangements may vary.
Feedback also highlighted the need to clearly distinguish the pre-screening from the subsequent
assessment phase. Further distinction between these two steps is established, noting that the pre-
screening is not explicitly mandated by the EHDS Regulation, but is recommended as a practical
implementation measure. In addition, the guideline now incorporates a clear flow diagram illustrating
the key steps, decision points and responsibilities.
Several stakeholders requested detailed, pre-filled example applications to support applicants. This
suggestion was considered but not taken forward, as the scope of this guideline is to provide
operational guidance for the HDABs rather than the applicants. Instead, the guideline includes field-
level examples indicating the type of information and level of detail expected in specific sections of
the application forms. Common issues encountered in practice are highlighted. Similarly, requests
to include predefined use cases were not addressed, given the wide diversity of national contexts,
data sources and application scenarios, which would limit the usefulness of generic examples.
Suggestions were also received to introduce simplified or accelerated procedures for ‘low risk’
applications. These were not incorporated, as the EHDS Regulation does not provide for

Annex 3 User journey
When a data useri applies for electronic health data for secondary use purposes, such
as research and innovation activities, education and policy-making, within the EHDS, the
user journey consists of several stages (see Figure 3). Access for certain purposes
(public or occupational health, policy-making and regulatory activities and statistics) is
reserved for public sector bodies and Union institutions (see Chapter IV, Art. 53(1) and
53(2)).
Figure 3: EHDS user journey consists of five main phases: data discovery, data access,
data preparation, use of data and finalisation.
Data discovery
Before being able to use the data, the user needs to investigate whether the data needed
is available and whether it is available in the necessary format for the secondary use
purpose. This phase is called data discovery. Datasets available in the EU can be found
in a metadata catalogue at https://qa.data.health.europa.eu/. Once the data discovery is
completed, the user can begin the process of applying for the data.
Data access
In the data access phase, the user fills in and submits a dedicated and standardised data
access application form or a data request to a health data access body (HDAB)ii. The
user must complete the information required in the form, upload necessary documents
and provide justifications as needed.

58
Guideline for Health Data Access Bodies on the procedures and formats for data access
Health data access application form is used when the user seeks to use personal level
data. Health data request is for cases when the user wants to apply for anonymised
statistical data.
Data preparation
During this phase, the data holder(s)iii deliver(s) the necessary data to the HDAB, which
starts to prepare the data for secondary use. Techniques for pseudonymisation,
anonymisation, generalisation, suppression and randomisation of personal data are
employed. The data minimisation principle (as per the GDPR) must be respected to
ensure privacy.
Use of data
In this phase, the user performs analyses based on the received data for the purpose
defined in the application phase. Analysing personal level data must be performed in a
secure processing environmentiv. The duration of this phase is specified in the regulation
(Art 68(12)).
Finalisation
This last phase of the user journey concerns data user’s duties regarding analysis
outcomes derived from secondary use of data. Data user must publish the results of
secondary use of health data within 18 months of the completion of the data processing
in a secure processing environment or of receiving the requested health data. The results
should be provided in an anonymous format. The data user must inform the health data
access body of the results. In addition, the data user must mention in the output that the
results have been obtained by using data in the framework of the EHDS.

Annex 11 Key recommendations for (electronic) contractual
arrangements
This annex contains key recommendations for the contractual arrangements needed between health
data holders and health data users in cases of data protected by IPR, trade secrets or covered by
the regulatory data protection rights (EHDS Article 52). Despite the specific content of such
arrangements will be further defined in dedicated EC documents and national guidelines on this
matter, this annex aims at providing insights on how these arrangements may be handled and
drafted.
EHDS Article 52 describes how electronic health data protected by IPR, trade secrets or covered by
the regulatory data protection rights should be handled and processed in terms of secondary use of
data. In health data context, protected content includes not only raw data but also:
- metadata describing this data,
- extraction algorithms and pseudonymisation workflows,
- harmonisation mappings and multi-centre alignment procedures,
- derived variables and model outputs (e.g., radiomics features, genomic annotations,
clinical scores),
- proprietary quality-control processes,
- internal or licensed analytical pipelines.
Such arrangement should result from an active conversation between the health data user and the
health data holder / IPR holder (if applicable, since health data holder and IP holder may be different
institutions). It is recommended to inform the HDAB regarding these arrangements, since HDAB is
the entity responsible for ensuring that the safeguards are adequate and feasible.
These contractual arrangements are intended to prevent health data users from attempting to infer,
reconstruct or reverse-engineer proprietary workflows, harmonisation rules or algorithms held by
health data holders. Their purpose is to protect the proprietary interests of health data holders while
ensuring that the potential of the protected health data can still be leveraged for secondary use,
provided that strict conditions are complied with by health data users. This is essential to safeguard
the competitive and scientific value of public research infrastructures. Moreover, if a health data user
refuses to sign reasonable contractual terms regarding the protection of trade secrets or IPR, the
health data holder has the legitimate right to advise the HDAB to refuse data access under Article
52(5). Additionally, health data holders must be able to identify datasets where sharing the data for
certain purposes would create a serious risk of violating IPR or trade secrets under EU laws. In these
cases, HDABs should accept this as a valid reason to refuse access [Article 52(5)].
These contractual arrangements are additional to and must not contradict the binding conditions set
out in the data permit or the data request approval issued by the HDAB. HDABs should consult the
health data holder before issuing permits or approvals involving IPR-protected datasets,
documenting the agreed safeguards and contractual conditions. If a health data user breaches the
